Enhancing Uber Pick-up with Community Data:
Uber Case Study

A concept redesign exploring how contextual data, community memory, and real-time guidance can eliminate the friction that causes riders and drivers to miss each other.

ROLEProduct Designer, UX Researcher
TIMELINE3 weeks
TOOLSFigma
PLATFORMiOS, Android
BACKGROUND & CONTEXT

The problem: Pickup Coordination, Location Precision, and Community Data

The pickup phase is the most fragile moment in any ridesharing trip. Despite accurate routing and payment systems, the handoff between rider and driver remains a significant source of confusion, delay, and cancellation. Users are frequently directed to inaccurate or inconvenient pickup points, and both parties lack the tools to close the physical gap between them. This project focuses specifically on three interconnected problems: – Location Ambiguity. GPS pins do not reflect real-world meeting points — streets, gates, and building entrances that matter to users are absent from map data, especially in cities with informal addressing systems. – Last-Metre Gap. Even when the driver arrives, riders cannot locate the vehicle — the app provides no active guidance to close the last 50–100 metres of the journey. .

🔍 User Research & Discovery

Analysis of recent App Store reviews surfaces a consistent pattern of pickup-related failures:,This problem does not affect all riders equally. It disproportionately affects users in specific geographic and situational contexts.

"Said driver arrived and I couldn't find him anywhere. Tried to call... wouldn't even allow it." — Tara DeMille, 1★

"The driver has driven past me without stopping and cancelled my trip. Then because it's cancelled, there's no way to complain." — Tianthee, 2★

"YET AGAIN I've been told to go and wait for an Uber at an unfamiliar location away from the venue I actually wanted to be picked up from." — Stuart Cormie, 2★

DESIGN SOLUTION

Putting clarity and control in riders' hands

💡 Key takeaway

– Riders at unmapped locations are no longer directed to distant pre-mapped points – Community pickup history provides relevant, proximity-ranked options sourced from real ride data – First-time locations are handled with a clear manual fallback that also generates future data – Pickup failure rates in informal addressing environments decrease as the community location graph grows – Driver-rider coordination on last-metre navigation is replaced by in-app guidance – Location names emerge organically from the people who use each point — without requiring any central curation – Over time, community pickup data fills the geographic gaps that standard map providers have not addressed
